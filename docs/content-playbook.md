## Page 2 - The Standard

### SHOOT THE ROOM. NOT JUST THE DJ.

You are not filming a DJ. **You are documenting a room reacting to a performance.** Warren drives the energy; the room proves it. WARREN WILL content is dark, warm, gold-lit, crowd-forward and slightly raw. If it looks like a polished corporate aftermovie, we missed.

### Workflow

Use one visual treatment for every workflow node; no unexplained filled-vs-outline state.

1. **CAPTURE** - 3 coverage phases
2. **SELECT** - keep the strongest moments
3. **GRADE** - dark + warm + gold
4. **DELIVER** - 24 h · 72 h · 7 d
5. **CROSS-POST** - TikTok · Instagram · YouTube

`24H SELECTS · 72H PHOTOS · 7 DAYS LONG-FORM`

How deadlines work: social content decays fast, so the default windows are 24 h selects, 72 h photos and 7 days long-form. If a window is not feasible for a booking, flag it before the booking is confirmed and we adjust together. Silence plus a late delivery is the failure mode.

### Coverage ratio

**70% CROWD** - faces · hands · reactions · the room

**30% BOOTH** - Warren · gear · mic moments

The labels and captions must align directly with their bar segments.

Closing line:

> Anyone can film a man behind CDJs. Only you were in that room, that night, with those people. Film them.

## Page 4 - Tech Spec

### VIDEO

- Master format: vertical 9:16 - 4K preferred, 1080×1920 minimum.
- Frame rates: 25/50 fps base · 60/120 fps for slow-mo (drops, hands, confetti).
- Shutter approximately double the frame rate (180° rule): 1/50 at 25 fps · 1/120 at 60 fps · 1/250 at 120 fps.
- Also grab 16:9 of 3-4 key moments for the YouTube recap.
- Whatever focus/exposure setup you trust - nothing that hunts or pumps mid-drop. No digital zoom, no in-camera filters.

### AUDIO

- If the venue or Warren can give you a board/recorder feed, take it - clean audio lifts the recap above phone footage.
- If not, camera/ambient is fine - the crowd roar is the product.

### PHOTOS + THE GRADE

- 20 edited selects. RAW files stay yours unless a handover was agreed.
- Dark, warm, contrasty. Blacks stay black - do not lift to grey.
- Push warmth toward gold `#C8A96E`; tame green and magenta LED casts.
- Natural skin tones. Grain is fine, plastic is not. No heavy preset packs.

## Page 5 - Pre-Set

### SHOT LIST A - CALL TIME 60-90 MIN BEFORE DOORS

Call time is confirmed per booking - earlier only if you are being paid for it. This material builds anticipation and opens every recap.

### MUST-HAVE

- **The walk-up** - 30 s before set start: Warren walking to the booth, from behind. This clip opens every recap.
- **Booth macro** - CDJ platters, faders, USB in, headphones on the mixer. Slow, shallow focus.
- **Doors / first hour** - people arriving, first drinks, energy baseline.

### WHEN IT HAPPENS

- Arrival / load-in - gear cases, the room empty and lit.
- **Soundcheck - Warren cueing a track with one headphone cup to one ear. 10 s is enough.**
- Warren prep - hands, headphones around the neck. Real moments, nothing posed.
- Venue details - signage, lights warming up, staff briefing.

## Page 6 - During the Set

### SHOT LIST B - THE 3-PHASE COVERAGE

Do not film continuously for 90 minutes. **Cover the set in three planned windows:** approximately 15 min at the start, a 20 min window mid-set, and the final 30 min where the energy peaks.

### PHASE 1 · OPENING - FIRST 15 MIN

- Must: first drop reaction - wide from behind the booth, crowd + Warren in one frame.
- Room filling up, energy baseline, early faces.

### PHASE 2 · BUILD - 20 MIN WINDOW MID-SET

- Dance-floor close-ups: faces, hands, couples, the one person losing it.
- FX details in slow-mo: jog wheel spin, fader push, knob twist.
- Mic moments - any time Warren talks, be rolling.
- Bar + edge-of-floor atmosphere - the full picture, not just front row.

### PHASE 3 · PEAK - LAST 30 MIN

- Must - the money shot: hands-up wide on the biggest drop - slow-mo, from the floor looking back **and** from the booth looking out. Both angles.
- Confetti / CO2 / lasers if the venue has them.
- Crowd singing back - close, on the faces.
- Final track and the exhale after it.

### FLOOR RULES

Never block the floor · no flash during a drop · keep changing angle.

**Guest phones:** do not automatically move just because a guest is filming. A phone can add useful foreground energy and make the moment feel real. Reposition only if the phone blocks the subject, duplicates your composition without adding anything, or ruins the intended frame.

## Page 7 - Post-Set

### SHOT LIST C - 30 MIN, THEN YOU'RE DONE

- **Must - Warren thank-you:** 10 s to camera, sweaty and real. Warm, direct, Bavarian; natural rather than scripted. One take.
- **Must - 3 vertical phone clips** of Warren for his night-of stories, before you leave.
- Promoter one-liner - "how was tonight?" Handheld; ambient noise is fine.
- Crowd exit - the afterglow is the proof.
- Break-down detail - USB out, headphones off, case closed. The full-stop shot for recaps.

### WHAT CONVERTS · APPLY CONSISTENTLY

- **Hook immediately** - open on the strongest visual or reaction, then give context.
- **Crowd-forward beats booth-forward** - the room is the story; Warren is the conductor.
- **Raw beats over-polished** - grade it, cut it, keep the sweat and human movement.
- **Personality beats production** - spontaneous crowd moments, local shoutouts and distinctive Warren details beat staged footage.
- **Captions for spoken words** - burn them into delivered masters: bold white, lower-middle safe area, corrected by hand. Do not use platform auto-caption styling.
- **Location adds context** - use one establishing shot when the venue, city, mountains or lakes genuinely help tell where the night happened.
- **One idea per clip** - one drop, one reaction, one moment. Save compilations for the YouTube recap.

This replaces unexplained shorthand such as "Servus energy," clarifies what "native captions" means in practice, and turns the previous "Munich is a character" line into a portable rule that works outside Munich too.
